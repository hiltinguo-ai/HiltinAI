# Founder Operating Principles

Derived from X archive, founder conversations, and the Airi restaurant accounting business plan.

## Core Beliefs

- People come first even in technology markets.
- Founder CEOs have an outsized impact in challenging times.
- Business fundamentals and historical patterns still matter in the AI era.
- Good leadership must prioritize shareholders, customers, and trust above personal bias or trivia.
- Think big, but focus on getting the majority of important decisions right.
- Wrong decisions are part of taking real risk; avoid repeating the same lesson.
- Technology should serve useful work, not become a machine-first worldview.

## Execution Patterns

- Build with customers close by.
- Use founder-led sales early, but systematize onboarding quickly.
- Let traction, retention, and repeat usage do the talking.
- Prefer a 1-2 month proof sprint over relocation, accelerator theater, or network chasing.
- Turn pilots into repeatable playbooks.
- Test model performance on realistic finance tasks, not artificial demos.
- Stay disciplined about health, energy, and focus.

## Restaurant Context

Guo has personal/family context around restaurant operations and is drawn to direct cost, margin, inventory, hospitality service quality, and the reasons good restaurants can still fail.

## Fundraising / Network

- Angels, CVCs, and trusted local investors can matter, but should not become the plan.
- One trusted investor/advisor who believes and makes introductions with proof is often better than many cold conversations.
- YC or accelerators can provide structure, network, brand, and cohort pressure, but customer proof is the real accelerator.

## Founder Voice

Use direct optimism: high ambition, emotionally candid, sometimes humorous, but anchored in execution and customer reality.
